# ZigZag 0.1.4

Corner handles that stay live, outlines as objects of their own, Simplify
Design, layers you can drag into order, and stitching that stays as it was
when you move things.

## Corners

- Select a shape and a small ringed circle sits inside each of its corners.
  Drag one towards the middle and every corner rounds, the stitches
  following as you drag, with the radius beside the pointer. Drag it back
  out for less, all the way for a sharp corner again.
- Click a circle first to pick that corner (it fills in), and a drag
  rounds just that one. Shift-click picks more; click the only one picked,
  or the shape, for all of them again.
- Rounded corners keep their circles: they stay corners you can round more
  or less later, also after saving and reopening, moving, rotating,
  scaling and copying. Stretch a rounded shape and its corners stay round
  rather than being squashed with it. Edit its points some other way and
  the rounds become ordinary points.
- Object › Path › Round Corners… works on the same corners: it opens on
  the radius they have, and a radius set there is the handles' from then
  on (0 makes them sharp). Inverted rounds and chamfers are still cut into
  the path.
- In Direct Selection, a click on a corner's circle selects that corner,
  so the next drag rounds it alone.

## Outlines

- Object › Path › Add Outline (and the task bar under the selection) gives
  a filled shape an outline as an object of its own, named "… (outline)",
  with its own thread and stitch type: a running stitch, or satin.
- A click on an outline's stitches selects its shape, whose task bar has
  the outline's settings in a second row; the outline by itself is picked
  from its row in Layers. Rounding the shape rounds its outline, and a
  dragged shape's outline moves with it as you drag.

## Simplify Design

- Object › Simplify Design… (or Simplify… in the Threads panel) makes a
  design quicker to sew: merge the threads most alike down to a number,
  take out details too small to sew well, sew across short gaps instead of
  trimming, sew each thread together, and, if you choose, unite layers
  that touch in one thread into one shape. It previews on the canvas as
  you drag, with threads, thread changes, trims, stitches and sewing time
  before and after; OK is one undo step.

## Layers

- The Objects panel is now Layers.
- Drag a row to another place in the sewing order: a line shows where it
  will go (before the row under the pointer, or after it), and the rows
  selected with it go too. A group's row takes all its layers. Escape
  calls it off.

## Text

- In a font menu (the task bar's and the Lettering panel's) the search
  field has the keyboard as soon as it opens, ↑ and ↓ step through the
  fonts, the text taking each as you reach it, and Enter closes the menu.
- Text with every letter deleted, and anything else deleted, is off the
  canvas at once; it no longer lingers or flashes back while the stitches
  are planned again.

## Moving things

- A filled shape, or filled text, is sewn the same wherever you move or
  turn it: the rows of its fill go with it. They used to be laid out on a
  grid fixed to the canvas, so every move changed where each part was
  sewn from, and with Connect on, which letters were joined. Designs made
  before this are sewn with their rows in slightly different places from
  now on, as dense as before.
- While you drag, the stitches that go with the move all follow the
  pointer: a shape's outline, and stitches that share a thread with shapes
  that are staying put.
- Rings whose stitches would lean (a triangle with a triangle cut out of
  it, a D) are sewn as columns cut at their corners.

## Saving and quitting

- Quitting with unsaved changes asks first: Save (Save All for several
  documents), Don't Save, or Cancel. Not saving loses nothing, as before:
  every tab comes back next time with its changes, and the files stay as
  they were until you save. The window's close button asks the same.
- The copy of your work kept as you go is written between gestures, not
  in the middle of a drag, a pan or a zoom, where it showed as a stutter.

## Crash logs

- If something inside ZigZag fails badly enough to stop it, a short log of
  what failed is kept (Help › Show Crash Logs), and the next launch offers
  to start a GitHub issue from it. Nothing is sent unless you post it, and
  people's names are taken out of the paths in it.
- About ZigZag is a window of ZigZag's own, with links to the project.

## For agents

- `add_outline` adds a satin or running-stitch outline to a filled shape,
  and `simplify_design` reduces a design's threads, small details and
  trims (`dry_run` says what it would come to).
- `round_corners` rounds live corners, so corners it rounds can be rounded
  again, or made sharp with a radius of 0.
